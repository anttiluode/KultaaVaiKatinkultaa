An experiment in native dynamics / 12 November 2026

Kultaa vai katinkultaa?

Gold or fool's gold? Eleven unchanged geometric units. One world signal. Eight circulating memory relays. Two signed error receivers. Watch a ring acquire, retain, and erase predictions through its own native pulses.

The period, initial wiring, and input schedule are supplied by the experimenter. The receivers and memory changes come from the original 2025 Perception Lab dynamics, not programmed Boolean comparisons or direct memory editing.

01 / A machine that can become quiet

The native write receiver fires when the world surprises an empty memory. Its pulse enters the ring at a carefully chosen point. On later laps the ring produces the expected event itself, making the receiver silent. The opposite signed receiver removes predictions no longer supported by the world.

02 / Four forms of the same unit

All source and receiver constants stay at 1.6. Only two coupling strengths change. In this arrangement the native unit becomes an XOR-like comparator, an AND-like coincidence detector, or one of two signed discrepancy detectors.

Each receiver is simulated independently for 44 ticks after settling, with two-tick input pulses. A positive source drive makes no external XOR calculation: it stimulates an ordinary native sender which then affects the receiver through its original setpoint port.

03 / What is and isn't being discovered

Demonstrated: signed native coincidence logic, a recurring learned phase in an initially empty 32-tick ring, and removal of an obsolete expected event using existing nonlinear dynamics. Not demonstrated: learning a period or general sequence law, robust phase-locking, physiological dendritic calcium mechanisms, or a maximal-length LFSR.

The same-sign XOR receiver goes silent through overshoot of a nonlinear firing band; that is not proof of physical destructive wave interference. This is a computational experiment in a sampled feedback system, not an optical bench experiment.
